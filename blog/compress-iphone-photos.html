How to Compress iPhone Photos (HEIC to a Smaller JPG or WebP)

Big files, and a format many sites reject. Here is the quick way to convert and shrink iPhone pictures.

By the Image Compressor Now teamPublished 2026-11-12Updated 2026-11-126 min read

Add them to a browser-based compressor that supports HEIC, choose JPEG for compatibility or WebP for the smallest size, limit the width to about 1600 pixels and download. Nothing has to be uploaded.

iPhone photos are sharp, but they are also big, and by default they are saved in a format many websites and programs cannot open. If a form rejects your picture, an email bounces or your storage is full, converting and shrinking the photo is the fix. Here is how to do it without installing anything.

Why iPhone photos are so large

Phone cameras capture many millions of pixels, so a single picture is often several megabytes. By default iPhones save photos as HEIC, an efficient format that stores the same picture in less space than a typical JPEG. The catch is compatibility: many upload forms, older Windows programs and some websites do not accept HEIC at all.

Which output should you choose?

OutputBest forNotes
JPEGForms, email, printing, older softwareWorks almost everywhere
WebPWebsites and modern appsUsually smaller than JPEG at similar quality
PNGScreenshots onlyLarge for photos, avoid

If you are unsure, pick JPEG. It is the safest choice for anything you are sending to someone else.

Step by step: compress iPhone photos in your browser

  1. Open the image compressor in your browser.
  2. Add your photos. HEIC and HEIF files are supported. They are converted to a standard picture first, then compressed.
  3. Choose JPEG or WebP as the output.
  4. Set a maximum width. About 1600 px suits sharing and uploads, and 1200 px suits email.
  5. Keep quality near 80% and read the new size on each result row.
  6. Download. Save single files, or use the ZIP button for many photos at once.

Your photos stay on your device. The conversion and compression run inside your browser, so nothing is uploaded.

Can I change the camera setting instead?

Yes. On current versions of iOS you can open Settings, then Camera, then Formats and choose Most Compatible to save new photos as JPEG. That fixes future compatibility problems, but JPEG files are usually bigger than HEIC, so you may still want to compress them. It also does not change photos you have already taken.

How much smaller will they get?

It depends on the photo, but a multi-megabyte picture limited to around 1600 pixels wide at 80 percent quality is commonly a fraction of its original size. Detailed or noisy scenes shrink less than simple ones. Check the result row, which shows the before and after size for every file.

Sending photos by email or messaging

Many email services limit attachments to roughly 20 to 25 MB in total, and messaging apps often recompress pictures on their own. Shrinking photos first keeps you inside the limit and avoids a double compression that can soften the image. For a specific small target such as 200 KB, follow our guide to compressing an image to 100 KB or 200 KB.

Remove location data before you share

Photos carry Exif metadata that can include the exact GPS position where the picture was taken. Re-encoding in this tool drops that metadata from the output file, which is a useful side effect when you post pictures publicly. The original on your phone is not changed.

Converting a large batch

You can add many photos at once, but phones have limited memory. If a big batch is slow or the page reloads, process 10 to 20 photos at a time. Do not compress the same file repeatedly, because every lossy pass removes a little more detail. Start from the original each time, and read our guide on compressing without losing quality if you want to keep photos looking their best.

Quick answers

Why will a website not accept my iPhone photo?

iPhones save photos as HEIC by default, and many websites and older programs cannot open that format. Convert the photo to JPEG, or to WebP if the site supports it.

Does converting HEIC to JPEG reduce quality?

JPEG is lossy, so a little detail is discarded, but at around 80 to 90 percent quality the difference is rarely visible at normal viewing size. Keep the original HEIC if you want a perfect copy.

Are my iPhone photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Conversion and compression run inside your browser, so the pictures stay on your device. A small helper script for HEIC conversion is downloaded only when you add HEIC files.

What size should I choose for email?

A maximum width of about 1200 pixels at roughly 80 percent quality is a good balance for email. Most photos will then be well under a megabyte.

Can I compress many photos at once?

Yes. Add them together and download everything as one ZIP. On a phone, work in groups of 10 to 20 photos to avoid running out of memory.
